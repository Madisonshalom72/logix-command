Lumper software: what crew-based logistics software must do

The short answer

Lumper software is operations software built for crew-based freight handling, not for calendars. Generic schedulers track shifts; LOGIX COMMAND builds the whole job — the org chart, the staffing math, the paperwork packet and the equipment list — then runs it across a 10-stage workflow with a load board workers claim seats on, training records, and labor math computed from rates the owner sets.

Why shift-scheduling apps fall short on a dock

The popular scheduling apps were built for retail and restaurant shifts: a fixed roster of employees, a weekly grid, open shifts to fill. Dock work doesn't run like that. A lumper operator's "schedule" is a stream of jobs — six trailers at one facility, a slab program at another — and every job demands a different organization: so many lumpers, a lead once the crew passes a size, a certified forklift operator on the big jobs, plus paperwork that changes with the freight. Software that only knows shifts forces the operator to do all of that thinking in their head, on paper, or in a spreadsheet that nobody else can see.

What the software must actually do

  • Build the organization for each job. Service line + unit count in; positions and headcounts out — including conditional seats (a safety lead who appears when the crew gets big) and certification-gated seats (a forklift seat only a certified operator may take).
  • Run a real workflow. Intake, site survey, quote and approval, contract and insurance, crew assignment, staging, execution, load-out, delivery confirmation, closeout and invoice — with progress that reflects paperwork completion, not just a stage label.
  • Generate the paperwork packet. Contracts, survey sign-offs, photo-condition logs, toolbox-talk sheets, per-load sheets and per-trailer unload logs, plus the employment or contractor documents matched to the crew type.
  • Post the work and let the crew claim it. A load board where open positions appear automatically and workers accept or release seats, with eligibility checked against their status, roles and certifications.
  • Train and record it. Role training with lessons and quizzes — dock safety, seals and counts, restack technique — tracked per worker, so a training gap is visible before it becomes an incident.
  • Intake the next crew member. A structured application (roles, certs, availability, background-check consent) that lands on the roster, not in an inbox.
  • Keep rates with the owner. Bill and pay rates per position, set by the operator, driving honest labor estimates per job — bill, cost and margin.
  • Carry the operator's brand. Name, tagline, colors and apply links, so clients and recruits deal with the operator's company.

Frequently asked questions

What is lumper software?

Lumper software is operations software for crew-based freight handling. Instead of just scheduling shifts, it builds the whole job: it converts a job's size into the crew the job needs, generates the compliance paperwork, posts open positions for workers to claim, tracks training and certifications, and follows the job from intake to invoice.

How is LOGIX COMMAND different from a generic scheduling app?

Generic schedulers track shifts — who works when. LOGIX COMMAND builds the whole job: pick a service line and a unit count and it generates the org chart, the staffing math, the paperwork packet and the equipment list, then runs the job across a 10-stage workflow with a load board, training records and owner-set labor rates attached.

Can lumper software run under my own company's brand?

Yes. LOGIX COMMAND includes white-label tools: the operator sets their own company name, tagline, colors, recruiting apply link and product attribution line, and the system — including its recruiting ads and generated documents — re-themes to that brand. Clients see the operator's company, not the software vendor's.

Does LOGIX COMMAND set my prices for me?

No. Every hourly bill and pay rate in LOGIX COMMAND is set by the owner and stays blank until they set it. The system computes job labor estimates — bill, cost and margin — only from rates the owner entered, so the software never quotes a number the operator did not choose.
